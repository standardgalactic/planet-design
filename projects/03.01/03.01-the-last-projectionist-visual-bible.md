# The Last Projectionist: Visual Bible

Catalogue slot 03.01 | Companion to the treatment, scene outline and screenplay | Target form: graphic novel, with a screen-test animatic as an option

---

## 1. Visual thesis

The book draws two kinds of image and keeps them apart: **what exists in the building** and **what someone says happened**. The reader should always be able to tell which kind of page they are on, and never be able to tell, from the drawing alone, whether an account is true.

- **Observed material** is hard-edged, charcoal, heavy black, with the grain of paper and film. Objects have weight, scratches and handling marks.
- **Reconstructions** are drawn inside one shared marker (section 5). The marker says "provisional, attributed to a speaker." It never says "false" or "true."
- **The one exception** is the closing screening, where the screen itself is the image (section 8).

Everything else in this document serves that rule.

---

## 2. Palette

The book is near monochrome with a small number of controlled colours. Colour is information.

| Role | Name | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ground | Booth Black | #1B1C1F | Shadows, booth interior, outlines on observed material |
| Paper | Leader White | #E9E3D4 | Page ground, labels, logbook, leader tails |
| Midtone | Cinder Grey | #6B6C70 | Charcoal wash, dust sheets, concrete |
| Projector light | Lamp Amber | #E7B04A | Beam, booth lamp, café window at dusk |
| Marker | Frame Silver | #9DB2B6 | Perforations and inner border of every reconstruction frame |
| Hot object | Van Red | #B3261E | The red van only (and the red of the exit signs, kept dimmer) |
| Pencil | Graphite | #3A3B3F | Annotations, handwriting, Priya's pad |

Rules:

- **Van Red appears in exactly three kinds of place:** inside reconstruction frames (as the van), on the actual screen in Scene 18, and as dim exit signs in the auditorium. It never appears on an observed object. This keeps the van a recurring mark of "someone's story" and lets Scene 18 land.
- **Frame Silver is used for the marker and nothing else.** No other element in the book uses it.
- **Lamp Amber** belongs to light sources only. It never tints a character's skin or clothing.
- Reconstruction interiors keep the same palette. They are not sepia, not desaturated, not blurred. Only the frame signals status.

## 3. Lighting by location

**Projection booth.** The home key is a bare work lamp at bench height, hard and slightly green-black in the shadows. The two projector ports throw thin amber shafts into the dark of the auditorium. Booth interiors are the highest-contrast pages in the book: crushed black, small bright objects.
- Day scenes: grey daylight from a single high window, dust in the air.
- Evening: the work lamp alone.
- Scene 18: one amber lamp, then dark.

**Auditorium.** Work lights (cold, flat, shadowless) for inventory scenes. House lights (warm, low) for public scenes. Projector beam for the final screening. Dust sheets over the seats in the middle acts read as pale hunched shapes; by Scene 17 they are gone and the house is bare.

**Pell's Café.** The warmest place in the book. Lamp Amber windows against a blue-black street, steam on glass, bright counters. Three light states: afternoon spill, evening lamps, closed-sign dark with one pendant. The cellar is lit by a single bulb, brick and damp, nearly monochrome.

## 4. Cast design

Design principle: each character has one silhouette cue, one hand cue and one posture cue, so they stay identifiable in panels where the face is small.

**Aurel Dacey (63).** Tall, narrow, slightly stooped from decades at a rewind bench. Grey cardigan over a work shirt, sleeves pushed to the elbow. White cotton gloves tucked in a back pocket. Hands are the star: long, precise, never fidgeting. Reading glasses on a cord he rarely uses. *Posture:* walks as if carrying something fragile. *Visual arc:* in Acts One and Two his lines are drawn clean and decisive; from Scene 13 his pencil lines on his own drawings become tentative and broken, with question marks.

**Priya Anand (29).** Compact, fast-moving. Municipal lanyard, dark parka in the cold auditorium, tablet always in hand, hair tied back with a pencil. Her face is often half lit by the tablet. *Posture:* leans in to read. *Visual cue:* she is often drawn at the edge of a panel, observing, while Aurel occupies the centre. By Scene 16 she moves to the centre.

**Margo Pell (74).** Broad shoulders, apron, a cloth always in her hand. Short grey hair, deliberate steps. She keeps a tea towel over one shoulder. *Posture:* stands behind counters and lets other people sit. *Hand cue:* she wipes surfaces when avoiding a subject.

**Benedikt Lowe (71).** Slight, cardigan, bow of the head as if waiting for the room to quiet. Hands folded. A pocket program from the old festival is rolled in his coat. *Posture:* leans toward whoever is speaking. *Visual arc:* the reconstructions he tells grow crowded with details Aurel supplied; by Scene 12 he is drawn with his own outline slightly broken, as if drawn over twice.

**Tobias Voss (58).** Upright, good coat that he rarely takes off, reading glasses on a cord that he removes when moved. Short grey hair, careful shave. *Posture:* stands at the end of a row, near an exit. *Visual cue:* always drawn near doors and aisles in Acts One and Two, seated in the house for Scene 18.

**Absent figures.** Edmund Hale and Daniel Wray are never drawn from life. They exist only as handwriting (Edmund's slanting, Daniel's round) and as figures inside reconstruction frames, where Daniel is a silhouette with no drawn face. Tobias's account is the only reconstruction in which Daniel is depicted at all, and even there only in outline.

**Costume continuity.** Each person wears the same silhouette every day (small variations only). It helps the reader track who is who in dense café scenes.

## 5. The reconstruction marker (specification)

Every reconstruction, whatever its source and whatever its truth, uses the same marker.

- **Frame shape.** A 35 mm-style frame with rounded corners and a perforation strip down both long edges (small rounded rectangles, spaced evenly, in Frame Silver on a Booth Black strip).
- **Inner border.** A thin Frame Silver line inset inside the frame that flickers: it is drawn as a slightly broken line with irregular gaps, changing from panel to panel but never from account to account.
- **Entry.** Frames do not simply appear. They enter by grafting onto an existing object: Aurel's pencil lines, Benedikt's hands, the café wall. The perforation strip grows outward from the source.
- **Exit.** Frames shrink back to the source object, leaving the source (a drawing, a mouth) in observed style.
- **Attribution.** A small caption in the gutter names the speaker: "Benedikt's telling," "Aurel's drawing," "Aurel's second account," "Tobias's account." The caption never uses the words "true" or "false."
- **Identity.** The frame edge, flicker and perforation spacing are identical in all accounts. A reader should not be able to rank the accounts by the marker.
- **Confidence is not shown by the marker.** Where an account is more fluent (Aurel's first), the drawing inside is faster and more detailed, but the border is unchanged.
- **Daniel's letter** is never inside a marker frame. It is an observed object: handwriting on paper.

## 6. Observed-object conventions

- **Leader and logbook pages.** Drawn at near life size, flat on the page, hard charcoal edge, no perspective distortion. The pencil annotation is lettered in the hand specified (Edmund slanting, Daniel round, Aurel tight).
- **The lab label.** Always shown in a wide panel among other cans, never in a dramatic close-up. The reader meets it as one object among many, as Aurel does.
- **The logbook entry.** Same: a flat page, centred, with the two added lines legible.
- **The count card.** Priya's tablet card sits in the top corner of each scene's first panel. Day number in Booth Black, open count in Graphite, on Leader White.
- **Film strips.** Drawn frame by frame with visible perforations. Observed strips use Booth Black edges; the strip edge colour never changes in a reconstruction. (Only the marker frame does.)

---

## 7. Layout and panel grammar

**Format.** Portrait trade graphic novel, 6.625 x 10.25 in, black-and-white with the controlled colour of section 2 as spot colour. Estimated length 140 to 160 pages.

**Grid.**
- Observed pages use a rigid 3-row grid with hairline gutters. Gutter is Leader White.
- Reconstruction pages use the film frame as the panel, with perforations in the gutter. The reader's eye moves along the perforated edge.
- A reconstruction may break the page grid, but the marker's perforation strip must remain legible.

**Camera.**
- Booth scenes favour low angles and tight crops on hands.
- Auditorium scenes favour wide establishing panels with a single figure small against rows of seats.
- Café scenes favour medium shots across tables with the counter in the background where Margo stands.

**Rhythm.** Act One pages are dense and efficient (6 to 8 panels). Act Two pages loosen as Aurel's certainty grows (4 to 6 panels, more silent panels of him drawing). Act Three tightens again and slows at the key records.

**Lettering.**
- Dialogue in a clean humanist hand with consistent weight.
- Handwriting on objects in three distinct hands: Edmund (slanting, narrow), Daniel (round, quick), Aurel (tight, upright block).
- Captions naming a reconstruction's source are small caps in a rectangular box tucked into the gutter.
- No sound effects except the projector's own rattle and the cello phrase, lettered as three rising ticks and a held fourth.

---

## 8. The closing screening (Scene 18)

The only moment where the reader sees an image from 1987 that is not a marker frame.

- The marker disappears. For four to six panels the page is the screen: silver, grained, hard-edged, with the beam of the projector crossing the top of the page.
- The red van appears in saturated Van Red for the only time as a screen image rather than a reconstruction.
- Audience reaction panels run in parallel: Benedikt's hand to his mouth, Tobias's glasses coming off, someone laughing and someone saying hush.
- The passage is confined to its 27 seconds. It does not extend into Daniel's departure. After the passage, the page returns to the booth.
- The cello is lettered as a visual device across the passage: three rising ticks, a fourth held.

## 10. Recurring motifs

- **Hands.** Aurel's hands, Priya's pencil, Margo's cloth, Tobias's glasses. Hands carry emotional state in panels where faces are small.
- **The count card.** A repeated panel in the top corner, changing only by number. It gives the book its clock.
- **Pillar.** The pillar in row C that blocks Benedikt's view of the back aisle appears in at least three auditorium panels before Margo mentions it, so the reader can check her objection.
- **Dust sheets.** Present while the story is conjecture, gone when the records separate.
- **Open and closed doors.** Café back door (to the old lobby), booth door, auditorium exits. Tobias stands beside one in every scene before Scene 14.
- **The question mark.** Aurel's first written question in Scene 13 is the only mark in the book that is not a statement. It returns in his final logbook entry as a form of discipline: the entry states sources, not conclusions.

---

## 11. Rules for what is never drawn

- No panel shows Daniel's face.
- No panel shows the festival night as direct memory before Scene 18.
- No reconstruction frame is coloured, tinted or edged differently to hint at an account's truth.
- No close-up isolates the lab label before Aurel notices it.
- No caption states what a character "really" thought. Captions name sources and objects only.
- No wholesale flashback to Harlan Voss. He exists only as a name on a register and a figure inside Aurel's first account.

---

## 12. Production notes for adaptation

- **Page count.** About 8 pages per scene on average, expanded for Scenes 05, 13, 16 and 18, compressed for 01, 04 and 06.
- **Reference studies to build first.** One booth interior with every object catalogued; one auditorium plan with seat and pillar positions; one café floor plan with the back room and cellar; a model sheet for each of the five characters; a specimen page of the three handwritings; one master marker frame with the perforation and flicker rules; one master count card.
- **Test pages.** Draw Scene 05 (marker grows from Aurel's drawing) and Scene 18 (marker vanishes into the real screen) first. If those two pages work, the system works.
- **Animatic option.** The marker and the closing screening are strong in motion. A 90-second test combining the two would validate the language before committing to the full book.

---

## 13. Open decisions

- Whether the book is printed in black and white with spot red and amber, or fully two-colour with Frame Silver as a separate ink.
- Whether the count card is drawn on every scene opener or only on the day change.
- How far to push the broken-line treatment of Benedikt's outline in Scene 12.
- Whether Edmund's slanting hand and Daniel's round hand are lettered by an illustrator or by a font drawn from sample pages.
