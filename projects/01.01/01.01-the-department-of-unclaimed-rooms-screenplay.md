# THE DEPARTMENT OF UNCLAIMED ROOMS

Screenplay, catalogue slot 01.01
Draft 1, built on the scene outline

---

## Conventions used on the page

- **TERMINAL HEADER.** Each scene opens on the register terminal's header: days until works begin, and the register's count of dwellings. It is an observed object, drawn hard-edged.
- **SECTIONS.** Building sections are drawn from one fixed viewpoint at one fixed scale. Each register has its own section. Slugs name which one is on the page.
- **No supernatural content.** Everything in the building can be measured. The strangeness is in the paper.

# ACT ONE: A MISMATCH

## 01. THE SCANNER

TERMINAL HEADER: DAY 30 / DWELLINGS 48

EXT. CALLOWAY HOUSE - MORNING

A six-storey brick building, once a hotel, with a lettered fascia still faintly legible above the lobby doors. Scaffold poles are stacked on the pavement. A hand-painted sign: REFIT WORKS COMMENCE DAY 0.

INT. CALLOWAY HOUSE, LOBBY - CONTINUOUS

ILSE VARGA (41), municipal lanyard, hard hat in one hand, sets a laser scanner on a tripod in the middle of the lobby. She levels it. She checks the level twice. A cheerful green light sweeps the walls.

GORDON PIKE (67), cardigan, a ring of keys the size of a fist, watches from the door of the caretaker's room.

GORDON
Takes long?

ILSE
A morning per two floors. It will want to be left alone while it thinks.

GORDON
Everything in this building wants that.

She follows the scanner's cable up the central stair, tape and tablet in hand.

INT. CALLOWAY HOUSE, THIRD FLOOR - LATER

Ilse stands at the head of the stair with the tablet. A model of the floor has built itself in grey point-cloud: corridor, eight door frames, the lift shaft.

SECTION: PLANNING REGISTER, THIRD FLOOR - eight flats along a corridor, the rear strip hatched flat grey.

She taps a measure. The volume of the point cloud, then the sum of the drawn rooms. A difference, small, the same on every floor.

ILSE
(to the tablet)
Recalibrate.

She recalibrates. The difference is the same.

## 02. THE SERVICE DOOR

TERMINAL HEADER: DAY 30 / DWELLINGS 48

INT. CALLOWAY HOUSE, SECOND FLOOR REAR - AFTERNOON

A service landing at the back of the building, lit by a single window onto a light well. Pipes along one wall. A narrow door painted the same cream as the plaster, with no handle on the corridor side, a small brass knob on this side.

Ilse follows the scan to where the difference must be hiding. She tries the knob. The door opens.

INT. ROOM 2R - CONTINUOUS

A room of eight square metres. A bed made tight, a kettle on a crate, a radio playing low, a small table. At the table, MAREK (50s), writing a letter with a ballpoint pen.

They look at each other. He puts down the pen.

ILSE
I am so sorry. I thought this was a store.

MAREK
It was. Then it was mine.

ILSE
Do you... live here?

MAREK
Since two thousand and nine. My partner is at work.

He reaches into a drawer without being asked and holds out an electoral card. "RM 2R, CALLOWAY HOUSE."

Ilse checks her tablet. The 1974 plan shows the strip behind the corridor as a hatched blank.

ILSE
(quietly)
It is not on the plan.

MAREK
(politely)
It is on the electoral roll.

## 03. FIVE ROOMS

TERMINAL HEADER: DAY 29 / DWELLINGS 48

INT. CALLOWAY HOUSE, REAR STRIP - DAY

A rapid sequence up the back of the building, each room entered by a narrow service door.

ROOM 3R. OTTILIE BRANDT (84), tiny, upright, in a cardigan buttoned wrong by one button. A single bed, a framed photograph of a man in uniform. She brings out a shoebox before Ilse can speak.

OTTILIE
You will want these.

Ilse opens the box. Letters, bills, bank statements. Most addressed to MR. A. BRANDT.

OTTILIE (CONT'D)
My husband dealt with the post. I only lived here.

ROOM 4R. DOLORES (60s), a radio and a crossword. A library card slid across the table.

ROOM 5R. TEODOR KASK (35), in a courier's jacket, opens a folder labelled with a pen. Six documents, each flagged with a yellow tab: bank, library, electoral roll, mobile account, a parcel firm's address check, a letter from a doctor.

TEODOR
You will need to see that they all say the same. They do.

ROOM 6R. JOSS (20s), earbuds out, hands her a utility bill.

Ilse measures each room with a laser. Her tablet logs the numbers: 7.4, 7.9, 8.1, 8.3, 8.6. All below 9. She pauses, in the last room, at the window. It opens onto the light well. She measures the well. 2.4 metres.

ILSE
(to herself)
Standard says three.

JOSS
Standard says what?

ILSE
Never mind.

CLOSE ON: Ilse's tablet. Five rows. Beside each, a field: DRAWN ON PLAN. The five boxes are empty.

## 04. THE LEDGER

TERMINAL HEADER: DAY 28 / DWELLINGS 48

INT. CALLOWAY HOUSE, CARETAKER'S ROOM - MORNING

A small ground-floor room with a hatch onto the street. Keys on a board, each hook labelled in marker. A kettle. A radio. Gordon sits at a desk with a ledger open, not looking at her.

Ilse stands in the doorway with her tablet. On the key board, five hooks are unlabelled.

ILSE
Mr. Pike, the rooms at the back. Do you know about them?

Gordon turns the ledger around and slides it to her.

GORDON
It is on my ledger.

She reads. Five lines, each with a date, a name and a sum. The earliest is 1979.

ILSE
You let them.

GORDON
They were doing nothing.

He opens a drawer. Handwritten slips, each with a room number and a signature. He says no more. Ilse, who does not yet know what the ledger is, does not ask him.

INSERT - THE LEDGER. Flat, hard-edged. Column headings: ROOM. NAME. SINCE. PAID.

## 05. THE PLAN IS THE PLAN

TERMINAL HEADER: DAY 27 / DWELLINGS 48

INT. DEPARTMENT OF BUILDING REGISTERS, REGISTER ROOM - MORNING

A basement. Flat files and plan chests. A terminal with a thirty-day header. DESMOND OKAFOR (59), tie, cardigan under his jacket, reading glasses on his forehead, pulls the 1974 conversion plan from a chest and unrolls it over a table.

DESMOND
The conversion. Nineteen seventy-four. Forty-eight flats.

He puts a finger on the rear strip. It is a pale hatched blank, unlabelled.

DESMOND (CONT'D)
That is outside the scope of the works. It is not drawn.

ILSE
It has rooms in it. Five. Occupied.

DESMOND
Are they real?

ILSE
I measured them. Seven point four to eight point six square metres.

DESMOND
Under nine.

ILSE
Under nine. Light well two point four.

He lifts the manual from its shelf and opens it. A thick, grey binder.

DESMOND
(reading)
"A dwelling is a space of not less than nine square metres, with a window onto a street or a court of not less than three metres, and a door onto a shared corridor."

He closes it, gently.

DESMOND (CONT'D)
The register records dwellings. These are not dwellings. They are, to the register, nothing.

ILSE
They have bank cards.

DESMOND
Proofs are easy to come by.

ILSE
Six offices?

DESMOND
Then six offices are mistaken. It happens.

He turns to the terminal. A line on the screen: REHOUSING PLAN, PHASE ONE, 10 PLACES.

DESMOND (CONT'D)
The decant plan is generated from the register. Eight households in the north stack, ten places, two spare. It balances.

ILSE
It balances because they are not on it.

DESMOND
It balances because the register is right.

## 06. THE WRONG PLAN

TERMINAL HEADER: DAY 25 / DWELLINGS 48

INT. HERITAGE ARCHIVE - AFTERNOON

A high-ceilinged room in a different building. Tall map cabinets. PRU HALDANE (50s), archivist, in cotton gloves, slides a long drawing from a drawer.

SECTION: HERITAGE PLAN, 1931 - the same building, the same stair, forty-eight guest suites and, along the back, five small rooms labelled STAFF.

Ilse lays the 1974 plan on top of it. The two drawings match almost perfectly, except for the rear strip.

ILSE
There.

PRU
Staff rooms. They were maids' rooms, I should think. Or porters.

ILSE
So the 1974 plan left them out.

PRU
It left them blank. That is not the same as leaving them out.

Ilse does not hear the distinction. She is looking at the five labelled rectangles.

ILSE
Then the plan is wrong. Rooms that exist should be on it. A wrong plan is a defect. A defect can be corrected.

PRU
(carefully)
What will you put them down as?

ILSE
As what they are.

PRU
They are staff rooms.

ILSE
They are habitable rooms with people in them.

She takes a photograph of the drawing. Pru watches her go. In the drawer, the 1931 plan sits open, unreconciled.

## 07. RESTORED

TERMINAL HEADER: DAY 22 / DWELLINGS 48

INT. REGISTER ROOM - NIGHT

The basement is empty. One lamp over Ilse's desk. On the terminal, the building's register, floor by floor. She opens the rear strip, which is hatched grey, and starts to draw.

Five rectangles, one per floor from the second up. She labels each: 2R, 3R, 4R, 5R, 6R. In the category field she selects HABITABLE ROOM. She enters the measured areas.

SECTION: PLANNING REGISTER, AS CORRECTED - for the first time the section is complete. Fifty-three rooms. Every strip filled in. Orderly. It is the last panel in the book that looks finished.

The terminal header ticks over: DWELLINGS 53.

She saves, switches off the lamp, and goes home with the particular tiredness of someone who has done a thing properly.

## 08. THE MORNING AFTER

TERMINAL HEADER: DAY 21 / DWELLINGS 53

INT. REGISTER ROOM - MORNING

Ilse hangs up her coat. The terminal has a row of red lines.

CLOSE ON: the screen. VALIDATION: 5 ERRORS. Each line reads the same: ROOM 2R. AREA 7.4 M2, MINIMUM 9.0. LIGHT WELL 2.4 M, MINIMUM 3.0. NON-CONFORMING DWELLING. CLOSING NOTICE GENERATED. 28 DAYS.

ILSE
(to the screen)
No.

She clicks. The notices have already been issued. A second window: REHOUSING PLAN, PHASE ONE, RECALCULATED.

Desmond comes in carrying two coffees. He sets one on her desk and reads the screen over her shoulder.

DESMOND
What did you do?

ILSE
I put them on the plan.

DESMOND
As what?

ILSE
Habitable rooms.

DESMOND
(gently)
The register applies the standard to everything it calls a dwelling. You told it these were dwellings.

He reaches past her and scrolls the rehousing plan.

DESMOND (CONT'D)
Phase one. The north stack. Eight households, plus 4R, 5R and 6R, because they are in the north stack. Eleven. Ten places.

ILSE
Who does it drop?

DESMOND
It ranks by length of documented tenancy and drops the last.

INSERT - THE RANKING TABLE. Eleven rows. A thin line drawn under place ten. Below it, one name: 6C. TENANTS SINCE 2024.

ILSE
That is the young couple. They have a baby.

DESMOND
They have the shortest paper.

INT. REAR LANDING, FIFTH FLOOR - MORNING

Teodor holds an envelope with a window. He opens it standing in the doorway of 5R. A single sheet, stamped.

INSERT - THE NOTICE. A closing notice. Same form, same stamp, same wording, five times over, stacked in a panel: 2R, 3R, 4R, 5R, 6R.

TEODOR
(reading)
"The above premises are unfit for human habitation."

His phone rings. A number he knows. He answers.

TEODOR (CONT'D)
Yes. Yes, I received it. No, it is not... no, I understand the policy.

TEODOR (CONT'D)
For how long?

TEODOR (CONT'D)

INT. REAR LANDING, THIRD FLOOR - LATER

Ottilie reads her own notice on the landing, standing, at arm's length. She folds it into quarters, then eighths. She puts it in her cardigan pocket and goes back into 3R.

## 10. 6C

TERMINAL HEADER: DAY 19 / DWELLINGS 53

INT. SIXTH FLOOR, FLAT 6C - AFTERNOON

A bright small flat. A mobile over a cot. NELL (28) and KOFI (30), the couple, on the sofa with a letter between them. Ilse stands near the door, hat in hand.

NELL
We got this yesterday. It says there are no decant places for us. Phase one starts on the day, and they cannot house us.

KOFI
We thought it was a mistake.

ILSE
It is not a mistake. It is... a consequence.

NELL
Of what?

Ilse looks at the cot. The mobile turns.

ILSE
Of something I did.

She cannot explain the ranking without describing her own correction. She tries.

ILSE (CONT'D)
There are people in the building who were not on the register. Now they are. The pool did not grow.

KOFI
So someone has to drop.

ILSE
Yes.

KOFI
And it is us because we are the newest.

ILSE
Yes.

NELL
(very quietly)
That does not seem like a reason.

CLOSE ON: the cot, the mobile, the pale shape of a sleeping hand.

## 11. WITHDRAWN

TERMINAL HEADER: DAY 17 / DWELLINGS 53

INT. REGISTER ROOM - MORNING

Ilse selects the five rooms and clicks RECLASSIFY. Category: STORE (ANCILLARY). The terminal header drops to 48. The red lines vanish. A message: CLOSING NOTICES RESCINDED.

Desmond watches from the door.

DESMOND
That was quick.

ILSE
I withdrew it.

DESMOND
You rescinded it. That is not the same as undoing it.

He sits on the corner of her desk.

DESMOND (CONT'D)
The rooms are stores again. The residents are in stores. The contractor has a schedule for forty-eight dwellings. There is no place in it for them.

ILSE
And Teodor?

DESMOND
His firm has noted the closing notice. They will not remove the note.

ILSE
(flatly)

DESMOND
Not where he was. Where he was with a note on his file.

SECTION: PLANNING REGISTER, RE-WITHDRAWN - the section returns to its earlier state. But the panel borders are very slightly out of register, as if drawn on the same sheet twice.

4R: Dolores, four proofs from three offices. TICK.
5R: Teodor, six proofs. TICK.
6R: Joss, three proofs, own name. TICK.
3R: Ottilie. She counts. Electoral roll, own name. Library card, own name. Everything else, her husband.

Two.

## 13. WHAT THE RULE LEFT OUT

TERMINAL HEADER: DAY 9 / DWELLINGS 52

INT. REAR LANDING, THIRD FLOOR - AFTERNOON

Ilse stands at the narrow cream-coloured door. She knocks. Ottilie opens it a hand's width.

ILSE
Mrs. Brandt. I need to tell you where we are.

OTTILIE
I think I know.

ILSE
The rooms on the plan are four. Yours is not one of them.

OTTILIE
No.

ILSE

OTTILIE

## 14. SEVEN REGISTERS

TERMINAL HEADER: DAY 7 / DWELLINGS 52

INT. REGISTER ROOM - NIGHT

The long table is covered. Ilse has been there since the morning. The 1974 plan, the 1931 drawing, an electricity company printout, the electoral roll extract, a pile of bank and library letters, Gordon's ledger on loan, a stack of tenancy slips tied with string, the rehousing plan.

She lays out a sheet of paper and rules it into columns. Across the top: REGISTER. RULE. THE 48. THE 5.

She works down the table, one register at a time.

ILSE
(writing, aloud)
Planning register. Rule: drawn on the plan, and meets the standard. Forty-eight, counted. Five, not drawn.

SECTION: PLANNING REGISTER - the front of the building complete; the rear strip hatched.

ILSE (CONT'D)
Heritage plans. Rule: any room on the hotel drawings. Forty-eight suites. Five staff rooms.

SECTION: HERITAGE PLAN - all fifty-three rooms.

ILSE (CONT'D)
Electricity. Rule: a metered point with an account.

She pulls the printout. Fifty-three service points. Five of them labelled SR-2 to SR-6.

ILSE (CONT'D)
(slowly)
Sub-meters. Since the hotel. Billed to the people in the rooms.

SECTION: ELECTRICITY - fifty-three meters in a column.

ILSE (CONT'D)
Electoral roll. Rule: a resident gives an address. Five rooms. Five of the six people are enrolled.

SECTION: ELECTORAL ROLL - the five rear rooms present, numbered.

ILSE (CONT'D)
Bank, library, mobile. Rule: accepts a stated address. Four of five rooms accepted. One refused.

Gordon's ledger. She lays it open.

ILSE (CONT'D)
Caretaker. Rule: key issued, rent paid. Five rooms since nineteen seventy-nine.

SECTION: CARETAKER'S LEDGER - five lines.

She sits back.

ILSE (CONT'D)
(to the table)
None of you is wrong.

She stacks the sections one on another. They almost fit. The edges do not agree, and the overlay is a dense, unreadable thing. Then she separates them, line by line, each in its own weight, and the thing resolves into seven separate buildings, all of them true by their own rule.

Desmond, passing through with his coat on, stops at the door.

DESMOND
You are still here.

ILSE
I have found that I was asking the wrong question.

DESMOND
Which one was that?

ILSE
Which register is right.

## 15. GHOSTS

TERMINAL HEADER: DAY 6 / DWELLINGS 52

INT. CALLOWAY HOUSE, SECOND FLOOR CORRIDOR - MORNING

Gordon with his ring of keys. Ilse with her tablet and the occupancy list. She runs her finger down the register.

ILSE
Two-D. Occupied. Tenant a Mr. Whitcombe, since nineteen ninety-one.

GORDON
Mr. Whitcombe passed in twenty twenty-one.

ILSE
It is on the register as occupied.

GORDON
Somebody has to fill in the form.

He opens 2D. A neat, empty flat. A mattress stripped to ticking. A window, light coming in on the bare boards.

INT. FOURTH FLOOR FLAT 4B - LATER

Another. A rented-out room with nothing in it. A rack of coat hangers.

INT. FIFTH FLOOR FLAT 5F - LATER

A third. A table with a stack of unopened mail from three years ago.

Ilse sits on the arm of a chair. Gordon stands by the window.

ILSE
Three listed flats, and nobody lives in them.

GORDON
They are sound. Heated. I turn the radiators on every winter, in case.

ILSE
In case of what?

GORDON
In case somebody turns up.

She opens a spreadsheet on her tablet. A line: PHASE ONE POOL 10. She adds 3.

ILSE
(speaking as she types)
Ten places. Plus three. Thirteen. Eleven households.

She looks up.

ILSE (CONT'D)
The register had ghosts. It was counting people who were gone, and it was not counting people who are here.

SECTION: PLANNING REGISTER, FLOORS 2, 4, 5 - three flats drawn with the same care as occupied ones. Over each, the register's occupant mark in a different ink: a small cross-hatched figure that is not there.

## 16. A NEW CLASS

TERMINAL HEADER: DAY 5 / DWELLINGS 48 + 5 OUT

INT. REGISTER ROOM - MORNING

Ilse stands at Desmond's desk with a single page.

ILSE
I do not want to alter a record. I want to add a class.

DESMOND
To the manual.

ILSE
Occupied, unclassified. It is not a dwelling and it does not get the standard. It carries the same rehousing entitlement as a classified household. It needs a physical survey and one independent record.

DESMOND
(reading)
And no closing notice.

ILSE
Not on the class alone. If there is a safety defect, that is a separate matter, and it stays one.

Desmond puts the page down.

DESMOND
A manual amendment is six weeks. The contractor starts in five days.

ILSE
I know.

DESMOND
And there is the question of what it permits. A class like this can be used. By people who would like to put others in sheds and call it a home.

ILSE
Yes.

DESMOND
Have you an answer?

ILSE
(after a moment)
No. I have a condition.

She takes the page and writes, in the margin: This class protects against invisibility. It is not an exemption from fitness.

Desmond reads it for a long time. Then he takes Gordon's ledger from the corner of his desk, where she left it, and turns it to the first page. 1979. A name, a sum. He reads each of the five lines.

DESMOND
It is a very good ledger.

ILSE
It is the only complete one in the building.

DESMOND
It would have made a better register than ours.

## 17. THE TEMPORARY DIRECTION

TERMINAL HEADER: DAY 4 / DWELLINGS 48 + 5 OUT

INT. REGISTER ROOM - MORNING

Desmond at his desk. A single sheet of paper, headed TEMPORARY DIRECTION. He reads it through, silently, once.

INSERT - THE DIRECTION. A short text. "Pending amendment, a class of OCCUPIED, UNCLASSIFIED SPACE is established. It confers rehousing entitlement equal to that of a classified household. It confers no exemption from fitness. Issued under the authority of the Registrar."

He picks up a plain pen and signs. He does it the way a man signs when he is aware that it is his own name.

DESMOND
(not looking up)
It will be reviewed.

ILSE
Yes.

DESMOND
By me.

ILSE
Yes.

He gives her the sheet.

INT. CALLOWAY HOUSE, CARETAKER'S ROOM - AFTERNOON

Gordon at his desk. Ilse sits across from him with a new form. The slips are in a pile.

ILSE
I need these as records. It means they exist, on file.

GORDON
And that I let rooms I should not have.

ILSE
It means that. There will be a fine.

GORDON
How big?

ILSE
Not large. Not nothing.

GORDON
Nineteen seventy-nine. A woman from the parish. She had a child.

Which room?

Four. She moved on in eighty-four. I did not charge her the last winter.

Nell opens a letter. A short one. She reads it twice and gives it to Kofi.

KOFI
(reading)
"Phase one place confirmed."

## 18. MEASURED

TERMINAL HEADER: DAY 1 / DWELLINGS 48 + 5 OUT

INT. REAR LANDING, THIRD FLOOR - MORNING

The narrow cream door, ajar. Ottilie stands in it, small, buttoned correctly today. Ilse has the laser measure.

ILSE
May I?

Ottilie steps back. Ilse enters, measures the length and the width, and reads the numbers aloud from the screen.

ILSE (CONT'D)
Seven point nine square metres.

She writes it on a form. The form has a line, printed in small type: UNCLASSIFIED. She crosses it out. Under it she writes, in her own hand: Measured. Occupied since 1982. Held by Ottilie Brandt.

She turns the form around. Ottilie reads it. She reads it twice, as she did with the notice.

OTTILIE
It says held.

ILSE
It does.

OTTILIE
Not "tolerated."

ILSE
No.

Ottilie takes the pen. She signs. The signature is small, upright, old-fashioned.

OTTILIE
My husband would have had a comment on that.

ILSE
I am sure he would.

INT. REAR LANDING - CONTINUOUS

Gordon is in the corridor with a screwdriver and a small box. He takes out a brass plate, two inches wide, engraved with a single character pair: 3R.

He holds it against the door, then drives two screws.

It is the first number that room has ever had on the city's side of the door.

INT. CALLOWAY HOUSE, LOBBY - CONTINUOUS

Ilse comes down the stair with the tablet under her arm. In the lobby, through the glass, a contractor's van is already parked for tomorrow.

She stops at the scanner, still on its tripod. On its screen, the full section of the building, every room, with a legend:

PLANNING. HERITAGE. ELECTRICITY. ELECTORAL. PROOFS. LEDGER. REHOUSING.

Beside each room, small marks show which register sees it.

CLOSE ON: the section. Five rear rooms, each with six small marks, one for each register that sees it, and one blank: the planning register, which does not.

CUT TO:

CLOSE ON: the brass plate on the door. 3R.

FADE OUT.

## Draft notes

- **Count arithmetic.** 48 flats plus 5 rear rooms is 53. Phase one: 8 listed households plus 4R, 5R, 6R is 11 households. Pool 10, then 13 after the three empty flats are counted. The header counts follow the register: 48, 53, 48, 52, then 48 plus 5 outside the dwelling count.
- **Day counts.** Day 22 correction C1; Day 21 notices; Day 20 Teodor suspended nine days (back Day 11); Day 17 withdrawal; Day 12 rule C3; Day 9 Ottilie excluded; Day 7 the table; Day 6 the empty flats; Day 5 the new class; Day 4 the direction; Day 1 the plate.
- **Registers.** Seven: planning, heritage, electricity, electoral, bank and library proofs, caretaker's ledger, rehousing plan. Scene 14 reads the first six aloud against the stack on the table. The rehousing plan is the seventh, and it is the register that depends on the planning register, which is the one that sees least.
- **Open for revision.** Length is a first pass. How much of the manual to quote, whether Desmond reads Gordon's ledger on the page, and the terminal's visual identity remain open.
